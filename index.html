Limelight Simulator

Pollen tracking, run away from the robot. The detector and the pursuit controller here are ports of what runs on the camera and the hub.

What am I looking at?

An FTC robot finding balls with a Limelight camera and driving to them. Nothing is faked: the simulator draws what the camera would see, then runs the real detection code over those pixels, and feeds the result to the real chase controller.

The three views

  • Camera view — the robot's eye. White circles are detections; the label is the range the camera thinks the ball is at. The green cross is the image centre.
  • Mask — the pixels that survived the colour filter, after the holes in the balls are filled in. This is all the detector has to work with. If a ball is missing here, it was lost at the colour stage, not the shape stage.
  • Field — top down. The grey square is the robot, the blue wedge is what the camera can actually see. Note the wedge does not start at the robot: there is a blind zone in front of it.

The one idea worth understanding

A camera tilted at the floor cannot see close to itself — the ball drops below the bottom of the frame. So the robot goes blind in the final few inches, exactly when it most needs to know where the ball is. That is why the controller commits: once a ball is close enough, it stops looking, drives straight for a fixed time, and calls it collected. The "Mounting" panel shows where your blind zone starts and warns if you commit too late to survive it.

What it cannot tell you

These pixels are clean. No motion blur, no camera noise, no gym lighting, no cardboard box that happens to look yellow. Every one of those has broken the real robot. Passing here means the maths is right, not that it survives a real field.
